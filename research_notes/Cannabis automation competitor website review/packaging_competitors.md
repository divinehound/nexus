## Which packaging competitors are most relevant to this business and how established are they?

### Takeaway
Measured against a business that combines automation with packaging, the most relevant competitors are **Custom Cones USA** (consumables plus filling and tube-packing machines in one store) and **N2 Packaging Systems** (packaging plus sealing equipment). **Gamut Packaging** (formerly MarijuanaPackaging.com) is the broadest packaging-supply e-commerce benchmark, **Calyx Containers** is the enterprise and innovation leader, and **Dragon Chewer** is the low-price pre-roll specialist that leans on content and SEO. Pollen Gear (Greenlane/KushCo) and Sana Packaging matter as product brands but now sell largely through distributors. Several of the suggested candidates could not be verified.

### Inferences
- **Custom Cones USA is the closest website analog to the target.** One storefront sells the machine plus consumables (cones, tubes, labels), offers bundle discounts and publishes deep expert content. If the target sells automation plus supplies, this is the model to benchmark first.
- Gamut is the breadth benchmark. It is a multi-brand distributor, so it competes on catalog depth and sampling rather than proprietary product.
- Calyx's packOS suggests the high end of the market is moving toward procurement software (proofing, approvals, reorders) rather than a classic cart. That differentiator mostly matters to MSO and enterprise buyers.

That implies a standard cart, live prices and Shopify checkout. This is an inference from URL structure, not a confirmed observation.

## What website features are table stakes in cannabis packaging e-commerce vs. differentiators?

### Takeaway
From indexed evidence, the table stakes are a shoppable catalog with case-quantity pricing, child-resistant compliance claims, samples, a custom-printing path and a resource blog. Differentiators are bundling equipment with consumables, state-by-state compliance content, sustainability product lines with credentials, in-house design services, and (at the high end) procurement and reorder software such as Calyx packOS.

### Cited Findings
- **Samples:** Gamut sells explicit sample SKUs ([sample listing](https://gamutpackaging.com/products/pollen-gear-child-resistant-wide-mouth-opaque-white-plastic-kush-canister-bottle-105dr-28g-sample)). ### Inferences

## Which sites are best-in-class for usability and conversion, and which are weak? Give specific examples.

### Takeaway
On the evidence available, **Custom Cones USA** and **Gamut** look strongest for conversion. Both have shoppable prices, bundles or samples, and deep content. **Dragon Chewer** is strong on SEO content and low entry price, but shows signs of catalog hygiene problems. **Calyx** optimizes for enterprise procurement rather than self-serve checkout. Pollen Gear and N2 appear weakest as direct self-serve storefronts, because their product reaches buyers mainly through resellers or sales-led projects. All usability judgments are **provisional**, because no site could be loaded.

### Cited Findings
- Custom Cones: transparent machine pricing ($1,999 King Kone 2.0) with bundle add-ons at the point of purchase, plus buyer-education content (machine comparison reports, a supplier-selection guide) — [King Kone 2.0](https://customconesusa.com/king-kone-2-0-pre-rolled-cone-filling-machine/); [Supplier guide](https://customconesusa.com/pre-roll-expert-blog/pre-rolls/how-to-choose-the-right-preroll-supplier)
- Gamut: sample-then-case path (separate sample SKU per product) and filterable brand collections ("Filter and sort") — [Sample SKU](https://gamutpackaging.com/products/pollen-gear-child-resistant-wide-mouth-opaque-white-plastic-kush-canister-bottle-105dr-28g-sample); [Pollen Gear collection](https://gamutpackaging.com/collections/pollen-gear)
- Dragon Chewer: low entry price ("as low as $0.04/unit"), free samples, many size and buyer guides. Weaknesses: duplicated "-copy" product URLs and very deep pagination (page 9+ of a single collection), which suggest discoverability friction — [Collection p.9](https://dragonchewer.com/collections/pre-roll-packaging?page=9); ["-copy" product](https://dragonchewer.com/products/116-mm-gold-pre-roll-packaging-box-pinch-n-flip-200-qty-copy)
- Calyx: price discovery requires a quote (about 1 business day via reseller GrowWorld). It is investing in packOS to digitize quote, approval and reorder for enterprise buyers — [GrowWorld](https://www.growworld.com/shop/products/45-dram-flower-edible-container); [Packaging Insights](https://www.packaginginsights.com/news/calyx-packos-ai-packaging-procurement-software-launch.html)

### Inferences
- Custom Cones is the best-in-class example for the target, because it sells *equipment and consumables together* with visible prices and bundles. The target should be measured against that experience.
- Quote-only pricing (Calyx, eBottles) conflicts with NN/g's finding that price is buyers' #1 information need (see next section). Mid-market buyers may bounce to sites that show prices, such as Gamut, Dragon Chewer and Custom Cones.
- Duplicate listings and deep pagination (Dragon Chewer) point to weak faceted filtering and merchandising. This is inferred from URLs, not observed in the UI.

### Gaps
- No page-speed (Core Web Vitals), mobile rendering, HTTPS-config, accessibility or checkout-step data could be gathered. Recommend PageSpeed Insights / BuiltWith checks once the domains are allowed.
- No independent reviews of site usability (Reddit, Google reviews) were retrieved for these competitors.

## General B2B equipment/packaging website best practices (Baymard, NN/g, Gartner) that apply to this niche

### Takeaway
B2B buyers increasingly want to self-serve: 61% (2025) and then 67% (2026) prefer a rep-free experience. They rank price as their top information need, and they punish inconsistency between the website and what sales reps say. For a combined equipment-and-consumables seller, that means visible pricing or price ranges, self-serve reorder and quick-order tools, consistent spec and compliance information, and easy access to an expert for fit questions on machines.

### Cited Findings
- 61% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free buying experience. 73% actively avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach (survey of 632 buyers, Aug–Sep 2024) — [Gartner, June 2025](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)
- 67% prefer a rep-free experience. 45% used AI during a recent purchase (survey of 646 buyers, Aug–Sep 2025) — [Gartner, March 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience)
- For tasks that need contextual judgment (does the product fit our needs?), buyers still prefer seller input. 69% report inconsistencies between supplier website information and what sellers tell them — [Gartner, March 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience); [Distribution Strategy](https://distributionstrategy.com/2026/03/most-b2b-buyers-prefer-rep-free-purchasing-as-ai-reshapes-sales/)
- NN/g: price is the #1 information need on any website, B2B included, yet B2B sites often hide it. Where exact prices are impractical, show prices for common scenarios or ranges — [NN/g "State the Price"](https://www.nngroup.com/alertbox/prices.html); [NN/g "Show Prices for Common Scenarios"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-prices-for-common-scenarios/). Secondary summaries report that NN/g found hidden pricing seen as evasive, and complex pricing tools rarely used — [Gravity Global summary](https://gravityglobal.com/news/blog/pricing-website-usability-opportunity) *(secondary source)*
- NN/g's B2B usability report is based on testing 293 B2B websites (findings paywalled) — [NN/g B2B report](https://www.nngroup.com/reports/b2b-websites-usability/)
- Baymard maintains 270+ B2B e-commerce UX guidelines covering product discovery, product pages, ordering and accounts. Its 2026 benchmark covers electronic components and machinery, the closest analog to equipment sellers (detailed findings paywalled) — [Baymard B2B research](https://baymard.com/research/business-to-business); [Baymard B2B machinery benchmark 2026](https://baymard.com/research-articles/b2b-electronic-components-machinery-ux-benchmark-2026); [Baymard B2B UX](https://baymard.com/industries/b2b-ecommerce-ux)
- Quick order (SKU and quantity entry, CSV upload, saved lists, real-time price and availability validation) is a standard B2B reorder pattern — [Elogic glossary](https://elogic.co/glossary/quick-order/); [Venue](https://mail.venue.cloud/news/insights/b2b-ecommerce-self-serve-ux-that-drives-revenue). A vendor blog claims a 30–40% increase in reorder frequency after adding quick order, but cites no source, so treat it as **unverified** — [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/quick-order-form-vs-traditional-cart-when-b2b-buyers-prefer-it/)

### Inferences (applied to cannabis packaging + automation)
- **Show prices** for stock consumables and at least starting prices or ranges for machines, as Custom Cones does. Use quote forms only for custom print runs and integrated lines.
- **Consumables drive repeat revenue.** Quick order, saved lists or one-click reorder, plus account order history, are the B2B best practice. Only Calyx (packOS) shows evidence of investing here.
- **Keep the website and sales reps consistent** on specs, lead times and CR certifications. Per-SKU compliance documents address both the 69% inconsistency finding and California DCC's guidance to request CR certification.
- **Hybrid support for equipment:** self-serve for consumables, plus an easy "talk to an engineer" or demo-booking CTA for machines, where Gartner says buyers still want seller input.
- **Samples as conversion bridge:** sample SKUs (Gamut) and free sample packs (Dragon Chewer) are the observed low-risk entry point for B2B packaging buyers.

### Gaps
- Baymard's and NN/g's specific B2B guidelines (e.g., exact quick-order or account findings) are paywalled. Only scope-level descriptions and secondary summaries were available.
- No cannabis-specific B2B buyer survey on website feature preferences was found.
